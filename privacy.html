Privacy policy

Your data stays in your store

Last updated: 21 November 2026 · Published by Ramniwas Saharan · Contact: ramniwassharan902@gmail.com

The short version. Packly runs inside Shopify. It has no server and no database of its own. It does not collect, send or store information about your store, your products or your customers anywhere outside Shopify.

What Packly accesses, and why

When you install Packly it asks for the access below. Each is used only to make packs work, and none of it is copied anywhere.

AccessWhat it is for
Products and variants (read and write)Creating and updating the colour and pack products you set up in Packly, and writing Packly's own settings onto them.
Inventory and locations (read and write)Setting the starting stock of a new colour at the location you choose.
Files (read and write)Attaching the photos you choose to a pack, and saving the backup file you ask for in your store's Files.
Publications (read and write)Making your pack and colour products visible on your Online Store.
Orders (read)Showing, on the order page in your admin, which packs an order contained and the items each was split into. Only the line items of the order you are looking at are read; nothing is stored.
Online Store themes (read)Checking whether Packly has been switched on in your theme, so the app can tell you if it has not.
Cart transforms and validations (read and write)Splitting a pack into its items at checkout, and refusing a pack that does not hold the right items.

Where Packly's own data lives

A pack's size, the variants a shopper may choose and the photo for each one are stored as app-owned metafields on your own products, inside your Shopify store. Three shop settings are stored the same way: the list of your styles, your style groups (the photo tabs), and the "packs only in collections" switch. When you uninstall Packly, Shopify removes the metafield definitions and keeps the values for a limited time that Shopify decides, then deletes them. The products Packly created are yours and stay in your store.

If you choose "Download backup" in Packly, a file with these settings is saved in your store's Files. It stays in your store until you delete it. Packly does not send it anywhere.

Customer data

Packly does not read, receive or store customer names, email addresses, addresses or payment details. On an order page it reads only the order's line items (which packs and items were bought), in your admin, and keeps nothing.

Its checkout logic runs as Shopify Functions, which by design cannot reach the network: they receive the cart, decide how to split a pack, and return. Nothing leaves Shopify.

When a pack is added to the cart, Packly writes what is in it onto the cart line, for example "2 × Black, M · 1 × White, L", so you and the shopper can both see it. That is part of the order and is held by Shopify.

The storefront parts of Packly (the pack choices, the style tabs and the home page section) run in the shopper's browser on your store. They set no cookies and send nothing to us. They may keep a short note in the browser's session storage (for example, that a list of colours was left open) which is never sent anywhere and disappears when the tab is closed.

Data requests and deletion

Shopify requires every app to accept three requests on a store's behalf. Packly accepts all three and verifies that each one genuinely came from Shopify.

  • A request for a customer's data. Packly holds no customer data, so there is nothing to provide.
  • A request to delete a customer's data. There is nothing to delete.
  • A request to delete a shop's data. Packly keeps nothing outside your store. Its settings inside your store are removed by Shopify after you uninstall.

Nothing from these requests is stored or logged.

Third parties

Packly sends no data to any third party. It has no analytics, no tracking and no advertising. This website does not use cookies or analytics either.

Changes

If Packly ever begins to store anything outside Shopify, this policy will be updated before that happens and the change will be described here.
